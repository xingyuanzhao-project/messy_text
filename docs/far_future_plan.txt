One of the central methodological challenges in human rights documentation is the development of workflows capable of recovering traceable evidence from large volumes of unstructured and heterogeneous textual data while producing reliable summaries that can be used for advocacy, litigation, historical memory, policymaking, and accountability processes. This challenge is particularly acute for human rights activists, non-governmental organizations, and victims, who often confront extensive collections of judicial files, testimonies, and administrative records that are difficult to navigate and interpret. This tool’s objective is not to recreate the original human coding process but rather to establish a transparent and auditable system that can extract relevant information, organize evidence, generate concise summaries, and incorporate prior annotations in a systematic manner. By structuring information according to crime-specific indicators and recording each stage of evidence extraction and summary generation, Conflibert provides a traceable framework that reduces the technical and financial burdens associated with large-scale documentation efforts while making legal and judicial information more accessible to non-specialist users.
At the same time, the system substantially reduces the labor required for repetitive tasks such as document cleaning, information extraction, reconciliation, and aggregation. Material that would otherwise require weeks of manual review can be processed in a matter of hours, allowing scarce expert labor to be redirected toward higher-value activities, including the validation of complex cases, the resolution of ambiguous classifications, and the interpretation of broader patterns of violence and human rights violations. The principal contribution of this approach is therefore not merely increased efficiency but a reallocation of human expertise toward analytical and interpretive tasks that remain essential to rigorous human rights research and documentation.
